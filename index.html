🕯 SOME CANDLES

A tabletop game of dwindling light and dark confessions — table companion

Some Candles is best played with 2–5 players plus a GM. Before you start: everyone should already have their Traits (virtue & vice), Moment, and Brink written on index cards, and the GM should have Their Brink ready. This app only tracks candles, the communal dice pool, dice rolls, and the action log — it doesn't replace character creation or narration.

Rules quick-reference

When to Roll

  • Call for a conflict roll whenever a character attempts something complex, risky, or with a genuinely unknown outcome, kicking a door, searching a ruin, fighting Them, sneaking past a threat, recalling a critical detail under pressure.
  • Simple, certain actions (walking down a hallway, picking up an already-found item) don’t need a roll — just narrate it. When in doubt, lean toward calling for a roll, since it's the main way players win narration and move the story forward.
  • Only one player rolls per conflict: whoever suggested the action or whose character is doing it. The same conflict can sometimes be reattempted by a different character for a different outcome, each attempt is its own roll.
  • The GM decides whether a conflict is a normal conflict, a Dire Conflict (see below), and whether a Moment or Brink applies — all of that is declared before dice are rolled.

Core Resolution

  • Conflict roll: roll the communal pool. Any 6 = success. 1s are set aside for the rest of the scene. No 6s = failure, a candle darkens.
  • Hope dice: succeed on 5 or 6, never lost to a 1. Only lost if a Brink reroll fails. Only 6s count toward narration rights.
  • Trait burn: once per scene, reroll only the dice that show 1 after a conflict roll. Only one Trait (Virtue or Vice) can be active at a time — burning the active one activates the other.
  • Brink embrace: reroll the entire pool. Success keeps the Brink (reusable). Failure burns the Brink, loses all hope dice, and darkens a candle.
  • Narration rights: most 6’s wins the right to narrate the outcome. Ties favor the GM. The GM always narrates a failed conflict.
  • Seizing narration: the active player may darken a candle to take narration away from the GM, even on a success.
  • Establishing Truths: after every darkened candle, speak truths equal to the new number of lit candles. Opens with "These things are true. The world is dark." and always ends with everyone saying "And we are alive." Only the GM may establish truths about Them, the dark, or the sunless sky.
  • The Last Stand: when only 1 candle remains, failed conflicts kill the character instead of darkening the candle. A player with no dice left in the pool automatically fails (and dies) without rolling.
  • Their Brink may only ever establish a strength or identity — never a weakness. Their only weakness is light.

Key Definitions

  • Dire Conflict: a conflict the GM flags in advance as carrying lethal or ruinous stakes if it fails (diving out of the way of an attack, a fall, a point-blank confrontation with Them). On a failed Dire Conflict, in addition to the normal candle darkening, any player, not just the one who rolled, may choose to have their own character die heroically instead of accepting the GM's outcome, taking over narration of the failure and the death. This is the only time a player narrates a failure. If the dying character had a Hope Die, it may pass to another survivor (Martyrdom).
  • Living a Moment: once a character's Moment card is active, the player may declare they're living it, then burn the Moment card before rolling a single conflict roll. Success inspires hope: the character gains a Hope Die and the player narrates how it goes. Failure loses hope: no Hope Die, and a candle darkens as normal. Either way, the Moment cannot be attempted again.

Ways a Candle Can Go Out

  • A conflict roll fails (no 6s, and no hope die shows 5 or 6).
  • A Moment roll fails (hope is lost).
  • A Brink is embraced and the reroll fails (the Brink also burns and all hope dice are lost).
  • A player seizes narration rights from the GM, even after an otherwise successful roll.
  • The candle simply burns down on its own over time, regardless of how the players are doing. The story always advances toward its end, one way or another.

Ways to Reduce the Risk of a Candle Going Out

  • Burn your active Trait to reroll any dice that landed on 1, giving a failing roll another chance at a 6.
  • Embrace an active Brink to reroll the entire pool for another shot at success, though a failed embrace burns the Brink and darkens a candle anyway, so it's a real risk, not a safety net.
  • Earn and hold onto Hope Dice; they add extra dice that succeed on 5 or 6 and are never lost to a 1, meaningfully improving your odds on every roll they're used on.

None of these prevent a candle from darkening on its own over time — there is no way to stop that. The rules are explicit that the end always comes.
